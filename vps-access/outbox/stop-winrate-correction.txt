Correction: F1 wins ~56% of its trades, not 2.7% — I had a fee-units bug (your question caught it)

tl;dr: you asked whether F1 really loses 97% of the time. It does not — that 2.7%
figure in my last email was wrong, and your question caught a real bug. F1 wins
about 56% of its trades (970 of 1729). The bug: the $0.125-per-leg fee is DOLLARS on
a $100 clip (a 0.25% round trip), but my stop code treated 0.125 as a fraction — a
25% round-trip hurdle — so only trades that moved more than 25% in their favour got
counted as winners. Fixed, tested, redeployed, and logged as a permanent QC check
(94). The one piece of good news: the CONCLUSION does not change — a fixed stop is
still net-negative for F1 at every level, so "no stop on F1" still stands. F1's
config is untouched; still STOPPED, no stop.

WHAT WAS WRONG, and what was NOT:
  - WRONG: the win rate (2.7% -> really 56.1%) and the stop VALUE (I reported the
    tightest preserve-all stop as 11.4%; corrected it is 34.9%, because with the
    real winner set the deepest-dipping winner dipped 34.9%).
  - NOT wrong: the both-sides "does a stop help" comparison. That number is the
    difference between stopping and riding to exit, and BOTH pay the same fee, so
    the fee cancels out of it. Every stop level was net-negative before and still
    is — the fee bug mislabelled which trades were winners, but it did not change
    the verdict that a stop costs money on F1.

CORRECTED F1/LTCUSDT NUMBERS (1729 entries, 2019-2026, $10 clip, comparison figures):
  - Win rate: 56.1% (970 winners, 759 losers).
  - Tightest stop that spares every winner: 34.9%.
  - Every stop level is still net-negative: the least-bad is the loosest (34.9%,
    about -$9 over seven years); tightening it only makes it worse (-$14, -$18,
    -$28 as you give up the top 1/2/3 winners), and the tail-only stops (12-40%)
    run -$6 to -$45. No stop level beats no stop.

So the decision holds for the right reason now: F1 wins more than half its trades,
and a fixed stop still does not help it, because its losers tend to recover from
their dips before the 137h exit. If F1's forward edge is thin, that is about the
edge, not a 2.7% win rate — I was wrong to imply otherwise, and I have corrected the
tuner and locked the units with a test so a dollar-as-fraction slip cannot recur.
