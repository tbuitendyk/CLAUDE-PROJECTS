My hypothesis was wrong, and the real answer is worse: frozen committees go SILENT out of sample
tl;dr: I said F2/F3 stopped trading because their breakout trigger became unreachable in calmer markets. Volatility did fall, but the trigger is still reachable in about 13 of 28 forward periods against 2 and 3 actual trades - so that explanation is dead. The real cause is upstream and applies to the whole approach: the committees stop calling a direction at all. Nothing is lost at execution. A frozen model does not fail loudly out of sample, it quietly starts saying "stand aside" forever while still returning a well-formed answer.

TABLE: where participation is lost, forward window, 28 periods. KEY:
"committee called" = periods where the members agreed on a side at all;
"trades" = positions actually opened; the last two columns split the
shortfall between the committee refusing to speak and execution failing
to fire.

  book  committee called  trades  lost at committee  lost at execution
  F1          14 (50%)        13                 14                  1
  F2           2 ( 7%)         2                 26                  0
  F3           3 (11%)         3                 25                  0

Against their own backtests, committee participation went:
F1 61.5% -> 50%   F2 73.8% -> 7%   F3 74.5% -> 11%

Individual members are close to mute. Out of 28 forward periods, F2's
four members called a direction 1, 0, 0 and 2 times. F3's eight members:
1, 0, 0, 0, 0, 1, 2, 0.

WHY THIS MATTERS MORE THAN THE TWO BOOKS. F2 came back as "2 trades,
-$0.51" - a small tidy loss that looks like an ordinary poor result. It
is not. It is a model that stopped working and said nothing. Right units,
plausible magnitude, no error raised: the exact defect shape this project
keeps meeting.

IT ALSO REFRAMES F1's GOOD NUMBER. Its +$27.43 rests on members calling a
direction only 3 to 9 times in 28 periods, with a 1-of-4 quorum turning
sparse opinions into positions. Thinner than the headline suggests, and
now recorded next to the headline rather than under it.

NEW HYPOTHESIS, declared before testing: F2 and F3 both use the
"directional" decision rule; F1 uses "argmax". All three thin out, but
only the two directional ones collapse. If the directional rule needs a
confidence forward data rarely supplies, that disqualifies an entire
decision family from forward use no matter how well it backtests - and
several candidates in the pipeline use it. The test is declared in the
audit, with its reading rule, and it comes BEFORE any further reading of
these books.
