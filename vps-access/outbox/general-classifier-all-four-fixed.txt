WHAT I FIXED

1. THE SAFETY CHECK THAT CHECKED NOTHING. It now sets off an alarm if it
   examined zero decisions while you are holding a position, and says why. It
   counts your open positions across the whole record rather than the last day —
   a trade is held for about six days, so a one-day window would have reported
   you as holding nothing. Paper trades count too and are named as paper: paper
   is the comparison arm, and an unchecked paper book is useless as a
   comparison, which is the only reason it exists. If you are holding nothing it
   stays quiet, because nothing is at stake.

2. QUIET DAYS ARE WRITTEN DOWN AGAIN. A day the members vote no now goes into
   the record like any other day. It carries the same votes, the same price and
   the same fingerprint as a trading day, so it gets re-checked like one too —
   this makes the check stronger, not just tidier. It is written once the entry
   price is known; if the price has not arrived yet the day is written on a
   later pass, and the system says out loud that it has not written it, rather
   than quietly skipping.

3. YOUR OWN SETUPS CAN NOW BE TUNED. This is the one that most deserved your
   complaint. The screen that scans for a protective stop level only ever
   offered three built-in research configurations. Your own setups — including
   the one holding real money — were not on the list at all, and the entry
   sitting at the top of it claimed to be the live one while pointing at
   something that no longer runs. Your setups are now on that list, first, under
   the names you give them, each scanned against its own training window.

   Underneath that, the scan used to quietly borrow its training cut-off date
   from those built-in configurations. So tuning a stop for your setup trained
   the models through a date belonging to something else entirely and handed
   back a confident-looking number. It now refuses to run unless it knows whose
   dates it is using, and it refuses immediately rather than after several
   minutes of loading.

   I did NOT delete those three built-in configurations. They are a record of an
   experiment whose dates were committed in advance, on purpose, so that a
   result could never be argued into something it is not. Deleting them would
   destroy evidence rather than free anything. What I removed is everything that
   silently treated their dates as everyone else's default. Tell me if you want
   them gone anyway and I will.

4. ONE ANSWER TO "WHICH COINS DOES THIS NEED". It was written out in four
   separate files and had already gone wrong once. One place now, and everything
   else asks it. If it ever cannot answer, the checks say UNKNOWN rather than
   printing an empty list, because "no coins" reads as "nothing to watch".

Also removed: the two leftover scheduled jobs from the old hardcoded setup are
now deleted from the machine rather than merely switched off.

HOW I CHECKED IT

Every fix has a test, and I broke each fix on purpose and watched its test fail
before trusting it — nine faults in total. A test nobody has seen fail is not a
test. The alarm in item 1 I also ran against the real machine rather than only
against made-up data, because a check that is right on invented data and blind
on the real thing is the exact failure this project keeps hitting: it turned out
the alarm needed the real records to be labelled a certain way, and I confirmed
all four of them are.
