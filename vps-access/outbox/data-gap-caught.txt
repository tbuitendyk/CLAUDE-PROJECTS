Caught: your running sweep reads to June 30, not Aug 2 — likely fine, here's why

tl;dr While building the planted-check button I found a defect in my
data-manager work: the coverage table counts the July/August day files,
but the sweep engine only reads whole-month bundles — and Binance has
not published July's bundles yet. So the table honestly says "to
2026-08-02" while your RUNNING sweep's data actually ends June 30.
The saving grace: the July 30 board you are retesting was built by the
same read path and ALSO ended June 30 — so your running retest is
span-matched to its target, which is exactly what a retest wants. My
recommendation is to let it finish. The fix is built and tested; it
ships with the planted-check release. Nothing needs doing unless you'd
rather stop the job.

What happened, plainly. Binance publishes each month's data two ways:
one big month file (published a few days into the next month) and
little one-day files (published daily). July's month files do not
exist yet, so when we refreshed, July and August arrived as day files.
I taught the coverage table to count day files — but never taught the
sweep engine to READ them. Two views of "what data we have" that
counted differently. No error anywhere; that is what makes this the
classic instrument defect, and it is mine.

What it means for the job running now:
- Its numbers are internally sound. Every window (train / test / hold
  / sealed reserve) is cut from data ending June 30.
- It is NOT stale relative to its purpose: the July 30 ATOM board you
  told me to retest also ended June 30. Same span both sides — the
  comparison is clean.
- Stopping it would gain nothing today anyway: the fix needs a service
  restart, which we never do while a job runs.

What I did (register entry 70):
- Wrote the test first and watched it fail for the real reason (the
  day files were invisible to the reader), then fixed the read path:
  a month with no published bundle is now assembled from its day
  files, everywhere — sweeps, refresh, downloads. Whole-month bundles
  still win when they exist.
- Rule going forward: any change to what the coverage table COUNTS
  lands in the same commit as the change to what the sweeps READ,
  with the test keeping them agreeing.

After your sweep finishes and the planted-check release deploys, a
one-click Global Refresh will not even be needed — the data already on
disk becomes fully visible to the next run, July and August included.
