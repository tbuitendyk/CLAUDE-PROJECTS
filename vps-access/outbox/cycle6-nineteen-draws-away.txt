You were right about the metric — cycle 6 away on the corrected one

tl;dr you caught me defending a measure I had already proved was broken, on
the grounds that I declared it first. That was stubbornness, not rigour, and I
have switched. The correct measure counts only test setups that actually
placed a trade, because one that never trades cannot tell us anything about
trading. On that measure our result is 56.6% against noise of 50.8% -- a
margin of 5.8 points rather than the 3.5 the old measure showed. Cycle 6 is
running to settle it properly: 19 scrambles instead of 6, because with only 6
the arithmetic caps how strong a claim is possible, and the cap rather than
the data was deciding the answer. Results around 07:00 UTC.


WHERE I WAS WRONG

I told you I would not switch measures because I found the better one after
looking. The distinction I failed to make:

  * METRIC SHOPPING is trying several measures and keeping whichever flatters
    you. That is what declaring a measure in advance exists to prevent.
  * METRIC CORRECTION is discovering your measure counts things that cannot
    possibly express the quantity you are asking about, and removing them.
    That is repairing a broken instrument.

Setups whose models never call a direction never place a trade. They cannot
win or lose a penny. Counting them in a score meant to detect tradeable
direction is not a defensible choice I had committed to -- it is a fault.

There is a clean test for which of the two I was doing, and I should have
applied it myself: the correction moved BOTH sides down, noise from 54.1% to
50.8% and our result from 57.6% to 56.6%. A cherry-pick only ever moves the
side you want moved. It also came from a mechanism argument rather than from
noticing a nicer number.

This is now QC register item 21, with the three conditions any future
mid-stream metric change has to satisfy, stated at the time: argued from
mechanism, moves both arms, old measure still reported alongside.

WHY 19 SCRAMBLES AND NOT 6

This is arithmetic, not method. If our result beats every one of N scrambles,
the strongest claim that supports is roughly 1 in (N+1).

    6 scrambles  -> best possible claim is 1 in 7  = 0.14
   19 scrambles  -> best possible claim is 1 in 20 = 0.05

At six, even a flawless outcome could not have reached the conventional bar.
The DRAW COUNT was deciding the answer instead of the data. Our result beat
all six, which is the best available outcome there, and still only earns 0.14.

So cycle 6 changes exactly one thing: 19 scrambles. Same universe, same
construction, same everything else.

To be clear this is not "keep running scrambles until one comes out right" --
that is precisely the trap this week has been about. The count and the
decision rule are both fixed in the launcher, in writing, before any number
exists, and the run gets read once.


THE RULE, FIXED IN ADVANCE

  GATE FIRST: the 19 scrambles must agree within 15 points of each other. If
  they do not, the instrument has broken again and nothing else is read.

  Then, on the active-only figure, against our 56.6%:
    beats all 19       -> claim of 0.05. The edge has survived an honest
                          test. It is then owed the MONEY question, which is
                          separate and harder: does the margin survive fees.
    beats 18 of 19     -> claim of 0.10. Suggestive. No live money on it.
    anything weaker    -> the aggregate edge closes. What is left is the
                          argmax/daily lead, which would need its own
                          declared test rather than another sweep.

  The old all-units figure is reported but decides nothing. If the two
  measures disagree, active-only governs and I will report the disagreement
  loudly, because that would itself be a finding.
