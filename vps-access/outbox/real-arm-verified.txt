WHAT IT DOES NOT YET TELL US, AND THIS IS THE IMPORTANT PART

Accuracy scores a wrong call the same way whether the market went nowhere or
went hard the other way. Money does not. A system can be right more often
than noise and still lose, if the times it is wrong cost more than the times
it is right earn.

So 1.61 points of accuracy is NOT a claim about profit, and I do not want it
read as one. On roughly 20,000 trades, fees alone are a large number.

CODE UPDATE (standing rule)

The money question needs its own census and I have built it. The numbers
already existed -- the simulator scores the chosen setup once on the
held-back slice, which is out-of-sample by construction -- but they never
reached the census, so money could only be read off the money-ranked
leaderboard. That is precisely the selection fault that invalidated our very
first screen back at job -2158: ranking by money and then reading money off
the top tells you about the ranking, not the market.

Every unit now records held-back P&L, trade count, wins, gross per trade, and
two do-nothing controls on the same slice -- always-long, and buy-and-hold --
so "did it beat simply holding" is answerable instead of assumed. Unfiltered,
every unit, never money-ranked. Tests pass; deployed with the next run.

WHAT I PROPOSE NEXT

The money version of exactly the test we just passed: net P&L after fees on
the held-back slice, real against the same 19 scrambles, with the reading rule
fixed before firing. Same shape, different question.

My honest expectation, stated in advance so it cannot be adjusted afterwards:
1.61 points of directional accuracy is thin, and I think there is a real
chance it does not survive fees. If it does not, that is a clean answer and
better than finding out with live money.
