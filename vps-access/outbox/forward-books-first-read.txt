First out-of-sample numbers: the leader is up $27 on 13 trades — which is below the floor I set, so no verdict
tl;dr: the forward books are built, deployed and read. On five weeks of data no run had ever seen, the money-screen leader (F1, LTC+XRP+BCH) made $27.43 on 13 trades, won 11 of 13, and beat both no-skill controls. I am NOT calling that a result: I pre-registered a 30-trade floor before any number existed and it has 13. The other two books barely traded at all, which is an instrument question I would rather raise now than explain later. Nothing here authorises a Binance trade.

WHY I AM NOT PLEASED ABOUT F1 YET, in order of force:
- Thirteen trades. Eleven wins from thirteen happens by luck roughly one
  time in ninety, which sounds strong until you remember three books ran
  and this is one draw of one five-week window. It is not a p-value and
  I will not quote it as one.
- The SIZE is a warning, not a selling point. $27 on a $100 book in five
  weeks is about 27%. A strategy whose own backtest managed $158 across
  eleven months suddenly running at that rate is more often thin data or
  a mistake than a durable edge.
- The 844% cost headroom comes from those same 13 trades and inherits
  their spread.
What IS meaningful: it beat both no-skill controls on a stretch where LTC
went roughly nowhere. That is the right kind of evidence. There is just
not enough of it yet.

F2 AND F3 RAISE A REAL QUESTION. Both traded about 0.75 times per period
in the backtest and 0.07-0.11 forward — a ten-fold collapse in how often
they trade. That is not small-sample noise, it is different behaviour.
My leading hypothesis, untested: both use breakout entries needing a
~2.4% move (1.5 x their frozen 1.61% band). If forward volatility is
lower than the training era, that trigger is simply out of reach and the
books stand aside almost always. F1 uses market entry - no distance to
reach - and did not collapse, which fits. Next check is declared: compare
forward volatility against the training era for XLM.

ENGINEERING DISCLOSURE, because you should not have to find this
yourself: I built this module badly three times before the first honest
read. Three times I read a field that does not exist, which produces
"undefined", vanishes from the JSON and crashes far from its cause. Worse,
the split used the 24-hour step as if it were the period's span, while
daily-4d does not close its trade until 138 hours after a period starts -
so about 5 training periods of 2397 had their outcomes inside the scoring
window. A real leak of exactly the kind the freeze exists to prevent,
fixed, and the test that should have caught it had copied the same wrong
assumption as the code, so it agreed with the bug instead of failing it.
All now covered by tests that assert against the geometry and round-trip
every reported field.

meanwhile.
