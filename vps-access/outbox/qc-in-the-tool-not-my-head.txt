A third confound, and the QC now lives in the tool

tl;dr you told me you want the quality control happening when you are NOT
watching. Fair, and the fix is not me promising to be more careful -- I have
moved the checks into the read tool so they run whether or not I remember.
Proof they work: pointed at last night's broken run they refuse to print the
comparison at all. Along the way I found a THIRD confound I was not looking
for: scrambling the answers does not just make the models wrong, it makes
them go silent. 2.4% of units never trade in the real run; about 15% never
trade in the scrambled ones. Those two things were never the same population,
and I had been comparing them as if they were.

In my last note I wrote that a number in the table was "cheap to check" and

But checking it turned up something I was not hunting for, which is worse.


THE THIRD CONFOUND: THE MODELS GO QUIET WHEN SCRAMBLED

A unit's committee can decline to call a direction. It says "flat" and stands
aside. It cannot then win or lose a penny.

But accuracy is scored on all three answers -- down, flat, up -- so a
committee that says "nothing will happen" every single period still gets
graded, and beats the baseline whenever the test window happens to hold more
flat periods than the training window's most common answer. It is recorded as
a WIN. It never placed a trade.

    real run (job -2211)        4 of 170 units silent    2.4%
    scrambled runs             ~15 of every 100 silent  ~15%

Six times as many. And the silent ones win far more often than average -- 58%
to 72% depending on the run -- so they are pumping up the scrambled side of
the comparison with units that barely exist on the real side.

Worse, the silence rate is not even stable between scrambles. Last night's six
draws ran 4.1%, 8.2%, 11.8%, 13.5%, 17.6%, 31.2%, and it tracks the result
almost step for step. So this was a second engine driving that 14.7%-to-91.2%
spread, sitting underneath the baseline fault I already fixed.

To be clear about what this does and does not mean: it does NOT say the real
result is fake. It says the comparison I was going to make was between two
different populations, and I would not have known.

THE ACTUAL FIX: QC THAT DOES NOT DEPEND ON ME

Every check below now prints on EVERY read, before any comparison, and the
tool refuses to show the comparison when a gate fails:

  * PARTICIPATION -- how many units never traded, how many of those are
    nonetheless counted as wins, and a warning above 5% naming the real run's
    2.4% as the reference.
  * A MUTE COLUMN per draw, so a silence rate that swings 4% to 31% is
    visible on the face of the table instead of needing a separate hunt.
  * ACTIVE-ONLY share printed next to all-units, always.
  * THE SANITY GATE at 15 points, the figure I pre-registered before firing
    the corrected run. Over that, it prints GATE FAILED and tells the reader
    to go back to the instrument.

Proof rather than assertion -- pointed at last night's broken job it says:

    *** GATE FAILED: the draws span more than 15 points. ***
    These are not draws of one statistic. The construction is still
    wrong. DO NOT read the comparison below.

A check nobody has watched fail is not a check, so I fired it at the run that
should fail it before trusting it.
