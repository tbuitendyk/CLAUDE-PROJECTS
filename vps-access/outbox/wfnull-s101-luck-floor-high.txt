WHAT EACH COLUMN SAYS, plainly:
- R1+R2, the headline: 7 real vs 5 by luck. The declared line (real must
  be at least double) is not met. On one draw, the real board's edge
  over luck is two setups — that is not evidence, it is a shrug.
- S1 is the useful embarrassment: 57 informationless setups "beat
  always-going-long". Mechanism: our picker chooses the best trade cell
  each fold, and cells that trade rarely — or stand aside — beat holding
  a falling coin no matter what the votes know. So "beats the market"
  was largely measuring the machinery's option to sit out, not vote
  skill. S1 is retired as a support rule (QC 64); from now on the luck
  arm itself is the yardstick every skill claim is measured against,
  and "vs always-long" is just a descriptive column.
