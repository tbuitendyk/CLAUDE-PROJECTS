THE STOP SCAN

Ran on your setup over its whole history: 2437 windows, 1804 trades, November
2019 to August 2026. 1014 winners, 790 losers.

TABLE — What a protective stop would have done, over full history
Each row is one stop level. "Winners cut" = trades that would have been stopped
out even though they ended up profitable. "Losers cut" = trades stopped before
their scheduled exit. "Net effect" = money gained by cutting losses MINUS money
given up by cutting winners short, on your $10 clip. Positive means the stop
helps. Every row is negative.

  stop level    winners cut   losers cut   net effect
  12%                    35          297      -$50.22
  15%                    17          205      -$39.36
  20%                     6          120      -$32.54
  25%                     1           69      -$20.33
  30%                     1           40      -$19.07
  32.163%                 0           30      -$14.22
  40%                     0           12       -$7.20

32.163% is the tightest stop that would not have clipped a single winner. Even
that one costs $14.22. The pattern is consistent: the deeper the stop, the less
it costs, and the least harmful stop is the one so far away it barely fires.

WHAT THAT MEANS

On this setup, positions that dip deeply tend to come back. A stop turns those
recoveries into locked-in losses, and it does that more often than it saves you
from a genuinely bad trade. That is why the engine currently runs with no stop.
The scan agrees with that, over seven years of data.

WHERE I THINK THAT ARGUMENT IS WEAK, before you rely on it

This measures the AVERAGE effect on profit. It does not measure the tail. A stop
is not really a profit tool — it is there for the one trade that goes badly
wrong, and this engine borrows to short, where an adverse move has no natural
ceiling. Seven years without such a trade is not proof that one cannot happen.

The thing already doing that job is the margin floor you set to 3. That is the
brake for the catastrophic case, and it is a better-shaped one: it responds to
how much trouble the whole account is in, rather than to one position's dip.

One more caveat I want on the record. The scan trains the committee through
June 2026 and then replays it back to 2019, so the early years are judged with
knowledge they did not have at the time. That is deliberate for a stop tune —
you want the deepest a winner ever dipped across all data — but it means the
"winners" are somewhat flattered. Flattered winners make a stop look WORSE than
it is, so the real cost is probably smaller than the table says. It does not
flip the sign; every level is negative by a wide margin.
