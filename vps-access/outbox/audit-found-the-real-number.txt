Step 7 amended — and the first audit under it cut our edge by two thirds

tl;dr you asked that step 7 force me to dig into a finished run, hunt for
weaknesses in the measurement myself, and insert fixes before the next step
whatever it was. Done, and it is now enforced by a gate: no new job fires
until the last completed one has a written audit. The first audit under the
rule immediately found the biggest problem yet -- our score was mostly
grading calls the system never traded on. Measured properly, our advantage
over noise is 1.9 points, not the 5.8 I reported an hour ago. It is still
positive and still above every scramble, but it is a third the size.

THE AMENDED STEP 7

  7. Analyse hard, audit the instrument, then loop.
     a. Go DEEPLY into the numbers and correlate them against the task stated
        BEFORE the run. Did it answer that, or a neighbouring easier one?
     b. ACTIVELY HUNT for weaknesses in the measurement -- do not wait to be
        shown them. What does the metric count that it should not, what does
        it omit, are the two arms the same population, and is any part of the
        number achievable with no skill?
     c. The next planned step is PROVISIONAL, ALWAYS. It does not proceed
        until (b) is fixed. Intervening steps are inserted as often as
        necessary, with no budget on them and no reluctance permitted.
     d. Write it to reports/audit-<jobid>.md. No new job fires without one.
     e. Every incorrect assumption found becomes a QC-REGISTER entry.

  Added underneath: "a defect found by the owner is a defect that already
  shaped a decision. The standard is finding them unprompted."

Enforced, not promised: require-previous-audit.sh refuses to launch when the
last completed run has no audit, and refuses a stub audit too, because a stub
reads as done.

WHAT THE FIRST AUDIT FOUND

Question 3 of the template is "what does the metric count that it should
not?", and the answer was large.

Our score graded all three answers: down, flat, and up. But "flat" means the
system stands aside and places no trade. So a setup that committed to a
direction ONCE and said flat for the other 130 periods was being graded
almost entirely on its flat calls. We were substantially measuring "can it
tell when nothing happens" and calling it trading skill.

The measure that corresponds to money is narrower: of the periods where it
actually committed to a direction, how often was it right?

TABLE 1 -- OUR EDGE OVER NOISE, THREE WAYS OF MEASURING IT
What it measures: how far real market data beats scrambled data, where the
scrambled data has nothing predictable in it. The gap is the whole point.

  measure                     real     noise     gap
  all-units share            57.6%     54.1%   +3.5 pts
  active-only share          56.6%     50.8%   +5.8 pts
  DIRECTIONAL ACCURACY       36.09%    34.23%  +1.9 pts   <- the real one

  KEY  all-units       = every test setup counted, including ones that never
                         traded
       active-only     = only setups that placed at least one trade
       directional     = of the individual decisions where it committed to
        accuracy         up or down, the share it got right
       real            = job -2211, live market data, held-back period
       noise           = job -0041, six scrambled runs
       (all figures are accuracy percentages, NOT money)

So the headcount overstated the effect roughly threefold.

WHY 36% IS NOT AS BAD AS IT LOOKS

Three possible answers -- down, flat, up -- so blind guessing scores near
33%, not 50%. Against 50% this reads as a catastrophe; against its own noise
level of 34.23% it is a small positive. This is exactly why I keep insisting
on comparing to the measured noise and never to a number that feels natural.

AND ONE GENUINELY GOOD PIECE OF NEWS

The six scrambles scored 33.40, 33.61, 33.88, 34.20, 35.13, 35.14 on this
measure -- a spread of 1.7 points, against roughly 13 points for the old
headcount. Our 36.09% sits above all six.

That tightness matters. The new measure pools about 20,000 individual trading
decisions instead of 170 setup-level verdicts, so it is far more precise and
can settle the question with far fewer scrambles.

It keeps running -- it does not need killing. Its records already include the

But its primary measure changes to directional accuracy, and I have written

before the numbers:

Honest disclosure recorded in the audit: cycle 6 was fired BEFORE this audit
existed, because the rule did not exist yet. Under the new rule the audit
comes first. It cost nothing this time by luck rather than design -- had the
audit demanded a different universe, five hours would have been wasted. The
gate exists so the next one cannot.
