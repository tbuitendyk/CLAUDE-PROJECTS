THE PRINCIPLE I HAD BACKWARDS

A gate failing means the MEASUREMENT is unreliable. It says nothing about
whether the idea is any good. So a failed gate must always mean "fix the
instrument and measure again" — never "bin the hypothesis".

I had been writing reading rules with clauses like "the edge direction closes"
and "the edge does not generalise", triggered by a single marginal
measurement. That hands an invented number the power to retire a candidate.
Which is exactly what you were pointing at.

Four rules now in CLAUDE.md:

  * No candidate is retired on one measurement. Retirement needs replication
    across periods, or a mechanism showing why it cannot work. "It missed my
    threshold once" is neither of those.
  * Every threshold is labelled DERIVED or GUESSED at the moment it is
    written. A guessed one is a prompt to look harder, not a verdict.
  * A gate that fails gets EXAMINED before it is obeyed. The money gate failed
    at 2.29 against 2.00 and turned out to be arithmetically incapable of
    passing.
  * Narrowing counts as retiring.

THAT LAST ONE, BECAUSE IT IS THE REAL ANSWER TO YOUR QUESTION

I checked all ten cycle launchers:

TABLE 1 -- WHAT EVERY RUN HAS SILENTLY EXCLUDED
What it measures: settings that were switched off in all ten cycles, without
ever being justified or revisited.

  setting                  value in all 10 runs   ever tested since cycle 1
  multi-asset combos       singles only           no
  trailing stops           off                    no

  KEY  multi-asset combos = using a second or third coin as context for the
                            traded coin (doubles, triples)
       trailing stops     = a stop that ratchets up behind a winning trade
                            instead of sitting still

Doubles and triples were in the original overnight sweep. Somewhere around
cycle 1 I set singles-only to keep the null comparison clean, and then copied
that line into every launcher for the next ten runs without ever asking again.
Trailing has never been on.

That is a stronger version of your complaint than the threshold was. A
threshold at least announces itself when it fires. A default that quietly
excludes a variant retires it more effectively than an argued rejection would,
because nobody notices it happening — including me, until I went and counted.

Neither was excluded for a reason that survives scrutiny. Both make the search
bigger and therefore make lucky-looking results easier to find, which is a
reason to test them carefully with their own nulls, not a reason to leave them
out for ten consecutive runs.

WHAT I AM NOT GOING TO DO

Throw them all into cycle 12 at once. Turning trailing on multiplies the
execution menu roughly twelvefold and doubles/triples multiply the number of
combinations; doing both together would produce a large, impressive-looking
board that I could not attribute to anything. That is how the first edge screen
went wrong.

One at a time, each with its own null and its own declared rule:

  after cycle 11   trailing stops, singles, everything else held fixed
  then             doubles, trailing held at whatever cycle 12 concluded

And both get the two-minute preflight first now.
