WHAT WENT WRONG, IN PLAIN TERMS

To sell short you have to borrow the coin first, sell it, and buy it back later.
The exchange has a convenience setting: "borrow whatever I'm short of." It sounds
like "borrow the whole amount." It does not mean that. It means "spend what's
already in the account first, and only borrow the rest."

Your account was holding LTC — four ordinary buy positions, still open. So when
the engine went to open a short at 01:20 this morning, the exchange looked in the
account, found plenty of coin sitting there, and just sold THAT. It borrowed
nothing at all.

Nothing was lost. The right amount was sold at the right price. But the engine's
records now disagreed with the exchange in two places at once: the account looked
short of coin against what the buy positions claim, and the loan looked too small
against what the short positions claim. Same missing 0.192 LTC, counted twice.
The safety check saw two faults and stopped the box.

WHY I SAY THE MONEY IS FINE

Add the two sides together instead of checking them separately:

  longs. "exchange" = coin actually in the account minus coin owed. Both in LTC.

That gap is the long-known scrap of coin too small to sell. Everything else lines
up. The check is examining the two halves separately when only the total is
meaningful, because the exchange keeps one pile of coin that both halves draw on.

THE SECOND, WORSE PROBLEM

The short that borrowed nothing can never be closed. Closing a short means buying
back what you owe — and the engine works out how much to buy by asking the
exchange what's owed. Nothing is owed on that leg. So on 29 August it will try to
close it, find no debt, and skip it. Every day after that too. Left alone this
does not recover on its own.

WHAT I CHANGED AND DEPLOYED

A short now takes out its own loan for the full amount BEFORE it sells, so the
debt always matches the position. If the loan is refused, no sell is sent at all.
If the loan goes through and the sell is rejected, the loan is handed straight
back, and if that fails the box halts rather than sit on a debt for a position it
does not have.

I also had to fix the test rig. The fake exchange used in testing was crediting
the full amount as borrowed every time — which is what the real exchange does
only when the account is empty. That was true in every test and false the moment
you hold anything. So 126 passing tests could not see this. It now behaves like
the real exchange. All four new checks were watched failing against the old code
first, and the main one reproduces exactly what happened to you.

130 tests pass. Deployed to the box, which is the safest possible moment: no new
positions can open while the halt is on, so the new code cannot do anything yet.
