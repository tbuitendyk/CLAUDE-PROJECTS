Plain version: the safety check on your two trades is checking nothing

tl;dr Fair hit on the jargon — "old rail" and "drift brake" are my words for
things you have never seen, and I used them without saying what they mean. Here
is the same message in plain English. One thing in it still needs your decision.

THE ONE THAT MATTERS

Your live setup has a safety check built into it. What it does: it takes a
decision the engine already made on an earlier day, re-does that decision from
scratch using the same data, and compares. If the answer comes out different,
something has changed that should not have, the recorded history can no longer
be trusted, and the setup is stopped from opening anything new. Its existing
trades still close on schedule.

Right now that check reports: nothing wrong, zero problems found, zero decisions
examined.

The last number is the problem. Every decision record your setup owns was
written by the previous version of the engine and is stamped with that version.
The check deliberately sets those aside rather than treating them all as
failures — that was the right thing to do, because otherwise all eight looked
broken and your setup got stopped this morning for no real reason. But once they
are set aside there is nothing left in the pile, so the check looks at an empty
pile, finds no problems in it, and says everything is fine.

So your two open trades currently have no such protection, and nothing will tell
you that. The trades themselves are fine and will close on their normal
schedule — this is about the watchdog, not the money.

There is already an alarm built for precisely this situation: shout if the check
examined nothing while a trade is open. It is attached to the wrong file. It
watches a file that the old setup used to write and nothing writes any more. The
alarm watching the file that is actually being written does shout about
mismatches, about errors, and if the file stops being updated — but not about a
check that looked at nothing.

It fixes itself the first time your setup makes a decision under its own
version, which is the next entry. I would rather tell you than let it quietly
resolve and never mention it happened.

Your call:
  1. Move the alarm onto the right file, so this is audible from now on instead
     of something I happened to spot.
  2. Leave it — it clears itself at the next entry.

WHAT I ACTUALLY CHANGED THIS MORNING, WITHOUT THE JARGON

The hardcoded setup used to have its own private set of scheduled jobs and
helper scripts, separate from the ones your setups use. That whole private set
is now deleted, and the installer wipes any leftovers off the machine rather
than just forgetting about them — a scheduled job left on disk keeps running
whether or not the installer still knows it exists.

Several of the checking tools I use to inspect the system had the names LTCUSDT,
XRPUSDT and BCHUSDT typed directly into them. They now ask your setups which
coins they need. One of those tools had been calling a piece of code I deleted
earlier, so it had simply been failing every time it ran.

I also got one of these wrong and caught it an hour later: my first version
looked for the coin names in the wrong place in your setup's record, found none,
and quietly fell back to reporting just one coin. It printed a healthy green
line for LTCUSDT while the other two went unwatched — the same fault as the
typed-in list I was replacing, just harder to notice. Fixed, and checked against
the real machine before I told you it worked.

TWO SMALLER THINGS, ALSO NOT TOUCHED

  * Days when the four voting models decline to trade are no longer written down

  * The question "which coins does this setup need" is now answered separately
