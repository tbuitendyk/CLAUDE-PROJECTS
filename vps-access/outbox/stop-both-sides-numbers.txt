The both-sides stop numbers you asked for — and they say F1 does not want a fixed stop at all

tl;dr: you asked to see, for cutting the top one/two/three winners, the effect on BOTH
sides — winners forfeited vs losers saved — in real money. Here it is, and it is
decisive: for F1 a fixed protective stop LOSES money at every level I can set it, and
loses MORE the tighter it gets. The reason is concrete — F1's losing positions mostly
recover from their dip before the 137h exit, so cutting them at the stop locks a
bigger loss than riding to the close. No stop, tight or loose, comes out ahead of no
stop at all. The tuner and the Bracket Lab control now show this whole comparison. So
the real decision is not "what stop" but "does F1 get a stop at all" — and its own
history says no. Nothing is set; the box is STOPPED with no stop configured.

The figures are per the live $10 clip, summed across all 1729 historical entries
(2019-11 to 2026-08), as a comparison — not the live book's realized P&L.

Every row of both tables is negative. Even a 40% stop — which only ever touches the
13 most extreme dips in seven years — still loses $6, because even those 13 recovered
enough by the exit that stopping them was worse. The least-bad fixed stop is no stop.

WHERE I PUSHED ON MY OWN NUMBER, so you are not taking it on faith:
  - This is pure expected P&L. It does NOT credit a stop for FREEING THE $10 CLIP: a
    position riding a long dip ties up one of your six slots; stopping it frees that
    slot for a new entry. If your book often sits at the 6-position cap, a stop has a
    capital/opportunity value my number ignores. (F1 rarely fills all six, so this is
    probably small — but it is the one real argument for a stop the table misses.)
  - It is an average over history, not TAIL protection. A stop still caps the worst
    single position, which has value beyond expected P&L even when the average is
    negative — that is what you originally asked it for ("runaway loss"). But the
    data says even 40%+ dips recover here, so F1's single-position ruin risk on a
    137h $10 clip is already small.
  - My "stopped" outcome is the stop LEVEL; a live stop fills at the next tick's
    market price, which OVERSHOOTS the level. So the real stop is a touch WORSE than
    every number above, never better. The finding is conservative.

MY READ: on F1's own full history, a fixed protective stop is counterproductive — it
does not avoid runaway loss, it manufactures realized losses out of dips that would
have recovered. I would not put one on F1 on this evidence. If you want tail
insurance anyway, a LOOSE stop (25-30%) costs the least (~$21-22 over seven years)
while still capping a genuine blow-up. Your call — and it may differ for a FUTURE
pair whose losers do not recover the way F1's do, which is exactly why the tuner is
now a general, per-setup tool.
