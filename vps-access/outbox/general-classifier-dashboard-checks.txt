Dashboard money now verified — and the first version of that check was useless

tl;dr Added a check that the Trading Dashboard's summed money matches the
records. Then I injected the exact fault it was meant to catch and it PASSED —
on a box with only one book, a wrongly-mixed total is the same number as the
right one. So there are now two checks: one for the arithmetic, one that
actually discriminates. 51 browser views clean, 505 unit tests pass, live pilot
untouched and healthy.

WHY THE DASHBOARD

Until now the numbers were verified on 2 of about 12 populated views. The most
valuable one missing was the Dashboard, because it SUMS realized, unrealized
and open across every book on the side you are looking at. A mixing mistake
hides best in a sum: one wrong book folded into a total is just a plausible
number, whereas the same mistake on a single setup's page shows a figure you
might recognise as belonging to something else.

THE PART WORTH TELLING YOU

I wrote the check, then injected the fault — the same real/paper mixing that
was found and fixed yesterday — and the check passed. This box has only a paper
book, so mixing both books gives exactly the correct total. The check could not
tell the two cases apart. If I had written it, seen green and moved on, I would
have recorded coverage that did not exist.

So it is now two things:

  * the totals check verifies the arithmetic against the records, and PRINTS a
    plain notice saying it cannot discriminate the mixing case on this data —
    so its green is never mistaken for proof of book separation.
  * a second check does the discrimination that works here: with a paper book
    open and no real channel, the real-money side must not show it. Break the
    filter and paper money appears under a live-money heading, which is the
    dangerous version of this mistake.

I also had two false alarms of my own, both caught only because I re-ran the
check after putting the fault BACK — a red run followed by a green run proves a
check moves, not that it moves for the right reason. First it demanded the live
side be empty and failed on the F1 pilot, which legitimately belongs there.
Then it looked for F1 in the wrong place: the page builds that row from the
pilot status, not from the config list, so my check was reading a different
source than the screen and disagreeing with correct behaviour. A false alarm on
the real-money screen is the worst kind — it teaches you to ignore the warning.
