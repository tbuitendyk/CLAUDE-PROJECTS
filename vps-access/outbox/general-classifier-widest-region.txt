Constructing: widest region is live — a second way to read the board

tl;dr — the board can now be ordered by how many NEIGHBOURING settings all made
money, not just by the single best score, and Greenlight has a third anchor,
"widest region", which confirms the middle of that run. Best cell keeps its
meaning everywhere; nothing was replaced. Deployed 19:12 UTC and verified. The
other half you asked for — permute ticks on the replication row with their own
null boards — is NOT built; it is the bigger half and I explain why below.

WHAT LANDED

Taking the top row of a board is how you find flukes: the best of about 1,260
tries looks good even when nothing is there. Noise gives you an isolated spike —
one good setting with bad ones either side. Something real gives a REGION.

So every promoted row now carries the largest set of settings that all made money
after fees AND all touch each other. Three rules decide what "touch" means, and
they are the whole claim:

- ONE step on ONE setting. A diagonal does not count. Letting a region hop two
  settings at once is exactly how scattered luck would read as a plateau.
- Steps are measured on the menu THIS run computed, not the library's full menu.
  A run with its own grid gets judged on its own spacing.
- entry and gate are categories, not an order. A market cell does not sit next to
  a breakout cell, so a region never crosses them.

Table: what the region reports per row.
Key — region: how many neighbouring settings all cleared the bar; bar: what a
setting had to do to count; centre: which setting the Greenlight would confirm.

  field    meaning
  region   the count — a lone winner scores 1
  bar      made money after fees, and actually traded
  centre   the DEEPEST setting in the region, walked in from its edge

That last one matters more than it sounds. The centre is not the region's
best-scoring cell. If it were, the shopped peak would be straight back in charge
wearing a different hat. It is also not "the one with the most neighbours" —
that cannot tell the middle of a line of five from its second cell, since both
have two neighbours. It is depth from the edge, and a region that runs off the
end of the swept menu is treated as edge there, because you do not know it keeps
going.

WHAT IT COST

Nothing to speak of. The sweep already computed every cell and threw all but the
winner away; this collects them and reads them once more. Cells are pooled across
agreement levels before the region is cut, because agreement is one of the
ordered settings — cutting per level would have missed every region that widens
as the committee threshold moves.

HOW TO USE IT

On the Boards section there is now an "order by" box: the board's own ranking, or
widest region. Same rows either way, only the order changes. The region column
shows the count, and hovering it names the centre and how many settings cleared
the bar out of how many were tried.

On the Greenlight section the anchor box now offers a third choice next to
declared cell and best cell. Pick "widest region" and the confirm runs on the
centre of that run of settings. If a row has no region — nothing on it made
money, or the run predates today — it refuses and says so rather than quietly
falling back to the best cell.

WHAT IS NOT BUILT, AND WHY

Permute ticks on the replication row, each with its own null boards number.

That one is not a UI job. Today a declared config is validated as ONE cell and
scored once per asset. Making it a SET changes what "declared" means all the way
down: the validator, the replication table, and the greenlight target all assume
a single cell. And per-tick null boards need something that does not exist yet —
the null machinery deals votes for a whole run, not for one setting at a time
with the rest held as the real run had them.

So it is a real build, not an afternoon, and I would rather it went in cleanly
than fast. Nothing about today's work blocks it or has to be undone for it.

Also unchanged, as agreed: the whole-run null boards number is still what limits
what you can claim. The per-tick numbers, when they exist, are for telling you
which tick is earning the result — not for multiplying together into a bigger
sounding one. Three ticks each passing at 1-in-11 is not 1-in-1,331; they are
computed on the same history and their odds do not multiply.
