Money null: my gate failed, and the gate itself was the thing that was wrong

tl;dr cycle 8 finished. Its declared gate failed, so I did not read the
comparison — and then found two mistakes of my own underneath it. First, I
had never run the real half with money recorded, so there was nothing to
compare against at all. Second, the gate I wrote was mathematically
inappropriate for this kind of number, and I proved that rather than assuming
it. One clean finding did come out: trading on pure noise LOSES money, about
$2,268 per pass. Any real result has to clear that, not zero. The missing real
half is running now, ~22 minutes.

The finding worth keeping: a system with nothing to predict does not break
even, it BLEEDS. Fees see to that. So "beat zero" was never the right target
and "beat noise" means beating roughly minus two thousand dollars.

WHY I DID NOT READ A VERDICT

My gate said the 19 scrambles had to agree within a 2-to-1 ratio of spread to
typical size. They came in at 2.29. Failed, so I stopped, as written.

Then, auditing it, two errors of mine:

1. THERE WAS NO REAL HALF TO COMPARE AGAINST. I deployed the money recording
   at 08:10 and the last real run was 07:29. So the 19 scrambles carried money
   and the real census did not. A five-hour run with nothing to measure
   against, and the cause was sequencing, not statistics.

2. THE GATE WAS THE WRONG SHAPE FOR THIS NUMBER. I first guessed the totals
   were being thrown around by a few large trades, and that dividing by the
   number of trades would settle it. I built that and measured: 2.25 against
   2.29. No improvement, and the arithmetic says it could not have been --
   every scramble makes roughly the same number of trades, so dividing by it
   is close to just changing the units, and a ratio does not care about units.
   I had reasoned by resemblance to an earlier fix without checking the
   resemblance held.

   The deeper problem: money-per-trade on scrambled data sits near ZERO by
   design. That is what a null IS. Dividing a spread by something near zero
   makes any spread look enormous. I had borrowed the gate from the accuracy
   test, where the null sits near 50% and a wild spread genuinely does mean a
   broken build. It does not carry over.

   The 19 draws are not unstable. They are a distribution, clustered below
   zero exactly as a fee-paying null should be.

THE REPLACEMENT RULE, AND WHY THE TIMING MATTERS

I am replacing the gate, not relaxing it, and I want the timing on the record
because that is the only real evidence of good faith available: THE REAL
MONEY NUMBER DOES NOT EXIST YET. It is being computed as I write. So the rule
below cannot have been chosen to fit a result I have seen, because I have not
seen one.

  The rank test, which assumes nothing about spread:
    real beats all 19 scrambles -> p = 0.05, the edge pays after fees.
    beats 18 of 19              -> p = 0.10. Suggestive. No live money.
    weaker                      -> the edge does not pay, and cycle 6 stands
                                   as a true statement about prediction that
                                   does not convert into money.

  Sanity check kept, and it is a real one: the scrambles must LOSE money. If
  a null ever shows a profit, the simulation is wrong, not the market. They
  lose, so it passes.

THREE NEW QC ITEMS (32-34)

  32  An analogy that worked once does not automatically transfer. Check the
      arithmetic before building on it. Cost: one wasted implementation.
  33  A gate that suits one statistic need not suit another. A
      spread-to-size ratio is meaningless for a quantity centred at zero by
      construction. Gates get derived per statistic, not copied.
  34  A census change does not apply to runs already made. After any change
      to what gets recorded, re-run BOTH arms on the same build before
      comparing. Cost: a five-hour run with no comparison.

All three came out of auditing my own run rather than being pointed out, which
is the standard step 7 now sets.
