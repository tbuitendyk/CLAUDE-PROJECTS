Plateau check: F1 is not a knife-edge — but my test was too easy to pass, and the decay across windows is still the real story
tl;dr: F1 sits on a plateau, not a spike. Its neighbouring settings all make money and all sit within 3x of it, so the cell was found rather than fitted to a razor's edge. Two honest caveats: every one of the 28 settings on the menu was profitable on that window, which made my "majority profitable" condition impossible to fail, so the pass is worth less than a passed test usually is. And the grid changes nothing about the number that matters most - the same cell earns $859 on the window it was picked on, $158 on held-back data, and $27 forward.

WHAT THE GRID ACTUALLY TELLS US, which is more useful than the verdict:
- The trade count never changes across a row. Market entry takes every
  committee call, so hold length only decides WHEN a position closes, not
  which ones open. That dimension is an exit knob, not a selection knob.
- Money climbs steadily with hold length to 137h then falls back at 161h,
  and the curve has the SAME shape in all four rows. That is what capturing
  a directional drift over five to six days looks like, not a sharp timing
  effect.
- F1 sits at the top of its row. Not a knife-edge, since its neighbours are
  close - but the top of a hill all the same, and picking the top of a hill
  still flatters the number. The row averages about $528 against F1's $859.

WHY I AM NOT BANKING THE PASS. All 28 settings were profitable, so my
"majority of neighbours profitable" condition was satisfied before the run
started. A gate that cannot fail tells you nothing. The only condition that
did real work was the 3x proximity one. And 28-out-of-28 is itself the old
lesson resurfacing: when every setting wins, the window is flattering
everything and profitability stops carrying information.

A STRONGER VERSION, for your decision: compare the SHAPE of this surface
between the search window and the held-back one. If the peak stays at 137h
the geometry is real; if it moves, the setting is fitted. That costs
spending the held-back window across all 28 cells, which is not free - so
it is worth doing once, deliberately, and only if you want it. I have not
run it.
