Why the shorts are not executing: the key is not allowed to borrow. One setting, yours to change.

tl;dr Every short since 24 August has failed before any order was sent, because
borrowing is switched off on the Binance API key. Nothing is broken in your
account and no money was lost or risked — the trades simply never happened. I
shipped a related code fix that was needed anyway, but it is NOT what is
stopping you, and no code can be: the key needs its margin borrowing permission
turned on, and only you can do that. Longs were unaffected throughout. I also
have to correct myself — my first read of the cause was wrong, and I say where
below.

WHAT YOU ARE SEEING

Your screen shows the calls being made. 21 September, a SHORT. 20 September, a
SHORT. Both were decided correctly and neither opened a position.

WHAT ACTUALLY HAPPENS, IN PLAIN TERMS

To sell short you must first borrow the coin. Since 24 August the engine has
asked the exchange for that loan as a separate, explicit request — which was the
right change, made after a short quietly sold coins from one of your buy
positions instead of borrowing.

The exchange refuses that request every time, with "you are not authorized to
execute this request". The engine then correctly sends no order at all, tries
again every ten minutes for an hour, and gives up. Six refusals a day, and then
the call expires.

    01:30   ask to borrow                       refused — not authorized

    02:20   call expires, abandoned             no order was ever sent

20 September is the same six refusals on the same clock.

WHY YOUR LONGS KEPT WORKING

A buy needs no loan, so it never touches the permission that is switched off.
Your long exits filled normally on both days — 20 September closed at $58.94 for
$1.15, 21 September at $61.68 for $2.01. That is why nothing looked wrong from
the money side: the half of the strategy that needs no borrowing carried on.

THE ONE SETTING

I read the key's own permissions from the box. It reports:

  WHAT THE API KEY IS ALLOWED TO DO
  Key: the permission as Binance names it, and whether it is on.

    read account data                     ON
    spot and margin trading               ON
    margin loan, repay and transfer       OFF     <- this one
    withdrawals                           OFF     (correct, leave it off)

Borrowing the coin is the third line, and it is off. Everything else the engine
needs is on. The account itself can borrow — the exchange told me you have 29.33
LTC of borrowing capacity available against a 1,300 limit — it is the key that
is not permitted to ask for it.

WHAT I NEED YOU TO DO

On Binance, where your API keys are managed, edit the key the box uses and
enable the permission covering margin loan, repay and transfer. I have not seen
that page, so I am describing the setting by what it does rather than quoting a
label at you — it is the one your key currently reports as off. The key is
locked to the box's IP address, which is right and does not need changing.
Withdrawals stay off.

I cannot do this and would not want to be able to: a key that can be given new
powers from a session is a key that can be given new powers by anything that
reaches that session.

Tell me when it is done and I will re-run the same read-only check and confirm
it from the exchange rather than assume it. The next short after that should
open normally.

WHERE I WAS WRONG, AND HOW I CAUGHT IT

My first answer to you was that Binance had deleted the web address the engine
was calling. Their own changelog says exactly that, dated for March 2024, and it
fit the symptom. I reported it as settled without checking it against your box.

Then I checked. The old address still answers normally. The refusal Binance
sends is its permission refusal, not its "no such address" one. And your journal
shows this very same key successfully borrowing 0.221886 LTC on 18 August, back
when the borrowing was bundled inside the order rather than asked for
separately — bundled borrowing rides on the trading permission, which is on.

So the cause is the permission, not the address. I have written this into the
quality register as a standing rule: name the evidence that would prove a
diagnosis WRONG, and go and get it before reporting, not after. A wrong cause
stated confidently sends you to fix the wrong thing.

WHAT I CHANGED AND DEPLOYED ANYWAY

The two web addresses the engine used for borrowing and repaying are deprecated;
Binance's supported replacement is a single address taking a borrow-or-repay
instruction. The engine now uses it. This was worth doing on its own and costs
nothing, but I want to be plain: it is not the fix for your problem.

More useful was what it exposed. The test rig was serving those old addresses
itself, so 130 tests passed over a borrow that could never work in production.
That is the second time in four weeks the fake exchange has been kinder than the
real one. It now refuses them exactly as Binance does, which makes four of the
existing short tests fail against the old code — as they should have all along.

WHAT COULD BREAK, AND WHAT SAYS IT DID NOT

Nothing about the deploy can disturb a position: your book held no open
positions when it went out, the update replaced a file without running a trading
cycle, and the engine stayed armed and unhalted throughout. The executor's test
suite passes 132 of 132, including two new checks I watched fail against the old
code first — one of them reproducing your production failure exactly.

The protections around shorting are all unchanged: a short still borrows its own
full quantity before selling, a refused loan still sends no order, and a loan
left behind a rejected sell is still handed straight back.

ANYTHING PREVIOUSLY REPORTED THAT THIS CHANGES

Yes, two things.

The note I sent on 24 August said the borrow-before-sell fix was "deployed" and
implied shorting was working again. It was deployed, but it has never once
succeeded against the exchange. Every short since has silently failed.

And the diagnosis in my message earlier today, naming the deleted web address as
the cause, is withdrawn. The cause is the key permission above.

ONE MORE THING WORTH KNOWING

This was invisible, and that is its own defect. A failed borrow is not in the
list of events that page you, and it does not reach the panel headed
"Incidents" on the profile's screen. The column headed "outcome" reads, on
those two calls, "intent shipped — awaiting fill", which is true and useless:
they were awaiting a fill that could never come. The only alert that could have
fired says "stale", which sounds like a timing hiccup.

Separately, the engine's "I am giving up on this trade, out loud" announcement
cannot fire on the current ten-minute schedule — the sixth refusal always lands
inside the last few minutes of the hour, so the call expires before the
give-up notice is reached. That is the announcement you asked for in August, and
it has never been able to run.

Neither of those is touched. They are screen and alerting changes on the live
rail and I am not making them unasked. Say the word and I will.
