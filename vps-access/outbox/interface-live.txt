WHERE EVERYTHING IS

Bracket Lab tab, under the Survivor board:

1. INSIDE A SETUP. Click "inspect" on any promoted row. You get:
   - the member table: each of the 12 models' OWN accuracy and edge on both
     windows, how often it commits to a direction at all, and — when the
     committee actually traded — how often that member voted with the trade.
   - the agreement matrix: for every pair of members, of the periods where
     both committed, the percentage where they agreed. Cells at 80%+ are
     highlighted red: those pairs are one opinion counted twice. This is how
     you see how many INDEPENDENT opinions "12 members" really is.
   Works on any run from today onward; older runs saved nothing and say so.

2. NULL TESTS. Pick a real run, pick a scramble run, pick a setup (or leave
   it at "board best"), press the button. Two verdicts appear with their
   rules printed beside them:
   - PER-SETUP: is this setup better than its own noise? Its real held-back
     dollars against the same setup in each scrambled world.
   - SELECTION-AWARE: is topping the board better than topping a NOISE board?
     Each scrambled world contributes its own best-of-170. This is the test
     that prices in the fact that the winner was picked after looking.
   Plus a sanity line: the scrambled population must mostly lose (fees). If
   noise ever profits, the simulation is broken and the panel says so in bold.

READING THAT HONESTLY

The pass says UNI-4d's result is not what that setup produces on nothing.
The fail says the STRENGTH OF BEING #1 is fully explained by picking the best
of 170 — a noise board's best row usually looks this good too. Consistent
with both: there may be modest real signal spread across the board (the
aggregate evidence we already have) without any single row being
distinguishable from a well-picked fluke.

Which is precisely why the freeze decision is yours to make with both numbers
in front of you, and why the forward paper test — not this window — is the
real judge of whatever we freeze.

One inspector detail from the same verification: this setup's individual
members are extremely quiet (member 1 commits on ~1% of periods alone), so
its trades come from rare moments of overlap, not constant opinions. Worth
seeing on the matrix yourself.
