The STOP button on Trading was arming the engine, not stopping it. Fixed.

tl;dr — read the first section before anything else. An exhaustive audit of the
Constructing and Trading tabs turned up 22 confirmed faults; all 22 are fixed,
tested and deployed (00:59 UTC), 495 tests pass and all 44 tab views load clean
in a real browser. One of the 22 is a real-money safety fault and you should know
exactly what it was.

1. THE MASTER SWITCH DID NOT STOP ANYTHING

On Trading → Live Trading → F1 → LIVE, pressing STOP engine sent a request to the
ARM endpoint with a field saying "armed: false". That endpoint has never read
that field. It always arms.

So STOP was not a no-op. It was a re-arm: a fresh request with a new timestamp,
which the box treats as a genuine START. You would have confirmed "STOP the F1
engine?", seen no error, and watched the screen redraw as RUNNING — and the
screen would have been telling the truth, because the engine was still running.

The old Live pilot page has always called the two endpoints correctly, so the two
pages disagreed and nothing noticed. No test ever pressed STOP.

Fixed on both sides. The page now picks its endpoint from the button. And the ARM
endpoint now REFUSES a request that says "armed: false", with a message naming
the disarm endpoint — so if anything ever makes this mistake again it fails
loudly instead of arming you. Both halves have been watched failing with the
fault put back.

What this did NOT do: it never armed anything by itself, and it could only have
mattered at a moment when you pressed STOP while the engine was running. I cannot
tell from here whether that ever happened; the journal would show it as an ARM
event at a time you meant to stop.

2. HOW THESE WERE FOUND

Everything that checked these two tabs read the SOURCE. Nothing had ever loaded
them. Two things now do.

A browser harness starts the service, opens both pages in Chromium and walks 44
views: every tab with nothing open, every tab with a saved run open, every tab
clicked through — disclosures expanded, rows clicked, every dropdown cycled
through every option, every button pressed except launchers and destructive ones
— plus the light palette. It fails on an uncaught exception, a console error, a
failed or 4xx request, a dead button (one whose handler is missing), an
undescribed column, and on undefined / NaN / [object Object] reaching the screen.

Alongside it, an audit ran 30 agents over both tabs from six independent angles
and then tried to REFUTE every finding rather than confirm it. 58 raw, 22
survived refutation. Those 22 are what follows.

3. THE OTHER TWENTY-ONE

Controls that could not work at all:
  - Greenlight anchor "widest region" was refused by the server. The one anchor
    whose purpose is to resist shopping was the one that could not mint a config;
    the two that worked are the two its own tooltip warns flatter themselves.
  - "No stop (clear)" sent a value the endpoint refuses. The protective stop
    could not be cleared from that tab at all.
  - "Copy settings into the form" threw on every click and filled nothing. The
    tab switched, so it looked like it had worked.
  - The null verdict could not be read for ANY three-asset setup, on either page
    — the endpoint dropped the context pairs. Since a config can only go live as
    a three-asset combo, the check that asks whether a result beats its own null
    has never been readable for anything we can trade.
  - "Fire null runs" fired a different instrument from the one it sat inside, and
    its output was displayed nowhere. Each round costs a full sweep.

Numbers that were wrong or absent:
  - The replication ranking was ordered by nothing. Its headline statistic was
    structurally impossible to compute, and with it always empty the sort
    collapsed — three configurations with plateau widths 9/5/1 came out 5, 1, 9.
    I shipped that yesterday. The tests I wrote for it read the comparator's
    source and passed.
  - History Tuning could never say PASS. Data fingerprint always blank. The
    Inspect member table's three columns always blank. The run-size equation off
    by a factor of twenty. The age-dial verdict printing "[object Object]".
  - Real and paper positions shown merged under one side's money.
  - The Setups table reported F1 as having no protective stop, whatever stop was
    applied.

Rough edges:
  - Trim's prompt pre-filled a date the server refuses.
  - The Data section offered TRIM on a fabricated exam pair, which would corrupt
    the exam silently.
  - Sweep started a new poller on every visit and never stopped the old ones.
  - Two tooltips were erased on load.
  - Run ids were typed into free-text boxes; they are dropdowns now. That is the
    same complaint you made about the stop tuner: "I thought I would be able to
    select from the saved sweeps."

4. AND THE COLUMN DESCRIPTIONS

You asked for column descriptions as tooltips. Trading had 67 column headings and
not one of them said what it held; Constructing had 9 of 97. 164 are described
now, with units stated — and in particular whether a number is money or accuracy
points, since that is the pair that gets confused. Every headline tile too. A
test fails the build on a bare heading, so this cannot decay again.

5. WHAT I AM NOT CLAIMING

The harness checks that nothing BREAKS. It does not yet check that the right
number appears — that a value on screen equals what the engine computed. That is
the next thing worth building, and it is the class that would catch a wrong
number rather than a missing one.

Four register entries added (146-149). The one worth reading is 148: a statistic
that decides an ORDER must be tested by executing the ordering, never by reading
its source. Reading the source is how the broken ranking shipped.
