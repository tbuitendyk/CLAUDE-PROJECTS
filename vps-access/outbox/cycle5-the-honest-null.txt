Cycle 5: the fix worked, and the edge does not survive it

tl;dr the corrected null ran clean and its six draws now agree within 9.4
points instead of 76.5, so the instrument is fixed. Read through it, our
57.6% is above all six draws -- but only by half a point over the top one,
and with six draws the strongest claim arithmetic allows is p = 0.14. That is
not significant. The bigger casualty: the null itself scored p = 0.0047 on
data it could not possibly predict, which means every binomial p-value this
project has quoted, including cycle 1's p = 0.027, was never valid. One real
lead survives, and weekly-8d -- what the live books run on -- comes out worse
than noise. Also: outbound mail broke for a few minutes tonight and part of
that was my doing; detail at the end.


THE INSTRUMENT IS FIXED

    job -2303 (old)   draws ranged 14.7% - 91.2%   spread 76.5 points
    job -0041 (new)   draws ranged 47.6% - 57.1%   spread  9.4 points

That is what a working null looks like: six independent scrambles landing in
the same neighbourhood. The gate I pre-registered was 15 points and it passed
at 9.4. 1020 units, zero failures, 94 minutes.

The tool confirmed the setting reached the run from the run's own record
rather than from the launcher that set it -- that check exists because a
dropped setting silently invalidated a conclusion earlier this week.


THE RESULT, STATED HONESTLY

    real run (job -2211)      57.6%
    the six null draws        47.6, 54.1, 54.7, 54.7, 56.5, 57.1
    mean of the null          54.1%

So the real result is above every draw. I do not want that phrase doing more
work than it can carry:

  * The margin over the TOP draw is 0.5 of a point.
  * With six draws, "beat all of them" is the best outcome available, and its
    p-value is 1/7 = 0.14. Even a perfect result could not have done better
    than 0.14 with this many draws.
  * Fitting a curve to the six draws puts 57.6% at about the 86th percentile.
    Same answer from a different direction: p ~ 0.14.

Conventionally that is not significant. My read: the aggregate edge is NOT
established. It sits at the top edge of noise, which is a reason to keep
looking, not a reason to trade.

Note also the null is 54%, not 50%. The statistic has a built-in upward tilt
of about four points. Anyone comparing a raw score against "50% = chance"
would have credited us with roughly four points of skill that are not there.
That is precisely what the whole null exercise was for.


THE BIGGEST FINDING IS ABOUT OUR P-VALUES

Look at what the SCRAMBLED run produced on its own group table:

    daily-2d argmax   65/102   binomial p = 0.0036
    POOLED            552/1020 binomial p = 0.0047

That is noise. There is nothing to predict in it. And it produced p = 0.005.

So the binomial p-values printed on every board this project has produced are
worthless -- the test assumes the 170 units are independent draws, and they
emphatically are not. Seventeen coins that all move together, and five chunk
shapes that are overlapping views of the same days. Cycle 1's "p = 0.0274"
was never evidence. I am retracting it.

The measured null is the only valid yardstick. This is the second retraction
in two emails, and both were cases of a number that looked like evidence
because it had the right shape.

The one pattern that holds up: all four ARGMAX daily rows beat their own
noise on median edge by a consistent +2.2 to +3.4 points. Same direction,
similar size, four times. The directional rows are erratic and mostly
smaller.

Honest caveat, and it is a big one: those four rows are not four independent
confirmations. daily-1d through daily-4d step forward one day at a time and
differ only in how far back they look, so they are overlapping cuts of the
same history. Four consistent readings of one thing, not four things. And
each real group is only 17 units, so the left-hand headcounts are very noisy
-- daily-4d argmax's +10.8 and daily-2d directional's -2.9 are both mostly
sampling noise.

WEEKLY-8d IS THE PROBLEM. It is the only family that beats nothing:
median edge 0.00% and -3.92%, no better than its own noise, and the worst
group in both runs. It also has the largest collapse from search window to
holdout -- searching finds +10.77%, holdout delivers 0.00%. On top of the
shrinkage finding from earlier (weekly-8d loses 10.8-13.2 accuracy points
search-to-holdout, the daily shapes lose 0.3) and its holdout being about 19
data points wide, that is now three independent reasons to distrust it.

Books 1 and 2 run on weekly-8d. I am not touching them -- the protocol says
frozen once live and I will not break that on my own say-so. But you should
know the geometry underneath them is the weakest thing on the board, and I
would not add money to it.


QC NOW RUNS WITHOUT ME

Per your point about wanting this happening when you are not watching, the
checks are in the tool and print on every read, before any comparison:
participation rate, a mute column per draw, active-only share beside
all-units, and the 15-point gate that refuses to show a comparison when it
fails. I fired it at the broken job first to watch it fail, because a check
nobody has seen fail is not a check.

It caught a third confound on its first outing: scrambling makes the models
go SILENT. 2.4% of units never trade in the real run; about 15% never trade
in the scrambled ones, and those silent units are scored as wins on their
flat calls alone. Two different populations being compared as one.

WHAT I THINK WE DO NEXT

Not more of the same. Six more draws cannot rescue a 0.5-point margin, and
running draws until one of them clears is exactly the trap this week has been
about.

The one thing worth a real test is the argmax/daily family, declared in
advance and on its own: fixed geometry, fixed decision mode, fixed quorum,
its own null, and judged on money after fees rather than on accuracy points.
Three points of accuracy is a different and much lower bar than three points
that survive a round trip in fees, and we have never yet asked the second
question of this signal.

I would rather put the box time into that one honest test than into another
broad sweep. Say the word and I will script it; if you would rather go a
different way, tell me and I will drop it.
