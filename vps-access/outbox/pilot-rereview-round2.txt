Pilot F1: two more adversarial reviews fixed and deployed — the box is ready to arm, here is what changed

tl;dr: since my last note I put the live pilot through two more rounds of hostile
review and fixed everything they found. The money maths and the arm-authentication
core were both confirmed CORRECT by the reviewers. What the reviews actually caught
was a cluster of "silent failure" holes — not in the trading, but in the WATCHER:
ways a dead box could look alive on the screen, or a refused START could leave you
staring at a spinner with no reason given. All closed, each with a test I watched
fail first. The box is still STOPPED and opens nothing until you press START.
Nothing here needs a decision from you except, when you're ready, to go live.

WHAT I DID, IN ORDER. Two review-and-fix passes, both deployed to the VPS and the
Mexico box, box kept STOPPED throughout:

  Pass 1 — the four "minor" items I'd flagged plus a re-check. Fixed a real money
  bug (see below), tightened the borrow-interest safety check, and reordered the
  hourly tick so the drift check runs before we ship a new decision.

  Pass 2 — a fresh three-angle hostile review (one reviewer each on: arming/liveness,
  money accounting, and alerting), each told to BREAK the code, not bless it.

THE ONE REAL MONEY BUG, now fixed. On a long trade the fee you pay to OPEN was
being silently dropped from the profit number. It cancelled out of the arithmetic
in a way that looked right, so every long round-trip was reported ~one fee too
profitable. The money reviewer re-derived a full trade by hand to the cent and
confirmed both the bug and the fix. This matters because the whole pilot exists to
measure whether the real fees leave any profit — an over-stated profit is exactly
the thing that would fool us.

THE SILENT-FAILURE HOLES the alerting/liveness reviewers found — all now closed.

  TABLE: what could have failed quietly, and what now catches it. KEY: "hole" = the
  state that used to produce no signal; "now" = the fix. All are watcher-side, none
  touch the trade path.

Plus: the START button now judges "is this request fresh" against EXCHANGE time
rather than the box's own clock, so a drifted box clock can't silently refuse you;
and a crash-recovered short trade now books an estimated borrow cost so its profit
can't be over-stated either.

WHAT I DID NOT "fix", on purpose — three accepted risks, all fail-safe (they end in
"stopped" or "false alarm", never "trading when it shouldn't"):
  - The kill switch trusts the delivery channel. It protects against the channel
    DYING (dead-man self-disarm), not against a channel that lies. Within "the VPS
    and its SSH link are trusted", that's the real boundary — worth you knowing.
  - A corrupted arm-record file could, in a sub-15-minute window, let a captured old
    START re-arm. Writes are atomic, so this is near-impossible in practice.
  - A backwards clock jump of hours could false-halt the book (a deadlock, not a
    loss). Both machines keep their clocks disciplined to internet time.

  2. Both clocks agree (the installer checks this; both read "synced" right now).
  3. The screen shows STOPPED until you press START. (It does.)
