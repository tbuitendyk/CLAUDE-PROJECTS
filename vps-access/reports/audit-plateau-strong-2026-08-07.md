### The two dimensions behave differently, and only one is stable
Hold length: consistent across both windows — longer is better, peaking at the
top end. That geometry is real.
Agreement level: NOT stable — 1-of-4 dominates the search window, 2-of-4
dominates the held-back one. The agreement setting F1 carries is the one the
search got wrong.

### The most interesting number in the grid, which nobody asked for
Degradation between windows is wildly uneven by agreement level:
  1-of-4  search $40..$859   held-back $-40..$158   (about 5x decay at the peak)
  2-of-4  search $59..$710   held-back   $6..$295
  3-of-4  search $80..$488   held-back  $19..$169
  4-of-4  search $51..$170   held-back  $48..$180   (essentially NO decay)
The most selective cells barely degrade at all — 4-of-4 earns roughly the same
out of sample as in it, across every hold length. The least selective cells
lose most of their money. That is what you would expect if low-agreement cells
are largely capturing drift that happened to run one way in the search window,
while high-agreement cells capture something that travels.
Their money is small (44 trades, $48-$180) but it is nearly identical in both
windows, which no other row can say.
This is a HYPOTHESIS generated by a spent window, not a finding: selectivity
may buy stability at the cost of size. It must be tested on fresh data with its
own declared rule before it means anything, and it must not become a reason to
quietly re-pick F1's agreement level.
