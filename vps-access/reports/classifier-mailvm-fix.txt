Classifier overnight #5 - the threading broke your mail for ten minutes. Fixed.

WHAT HAPPENED

The overnight #4 email would not send. Both SMTP ports timed out - connect
succeeded, then the session hung and died at 25 seconds. Same on a retry.

The mail VM was not down. Ping fine, ports 25/587/465/143/993 all open, the
VirtualBoxVM process running normally. But SSH into the guest was taking 12 to
23 seconds and one attempt timed out during the banner exchange. That is a
machine that is alive and starved, not a machine that has fallen over.

The cause is the threading I deployed tonight. Three worker threads at full
tilt on a four-core box leave the mail VM nothing to be interactive with. The
CPU cap does not help here and I should have seen that when I wrote it: the
cap is a DUTY CYCLE, so the workers still run at full priority during every
busy slice - they just have gaps. Postfix does not care about the gaps, it
cares about whether it gets scheduled inside its timeout.

Mail was degraded from roughly 09:59 to 10:07, i.e. the length of one job.

WHERE I WENT WRONG DIAGNOSING IT

I cancelled the running job on the starvation hypothesis before I had any host
data. Then, when the very next send attempt also failed, I concluded the
hypothesis was wrong - that was too hasty in the other direction, because the
VM needs a few minutes to dig out once the load stops. It sent fine on the
attempt after that.

Net cost: about ten minutes of compute on a cancelled diagnostic run, and one
wrong conclusion I had to withdraw a few minutes later. The right order was
host data first, hypothesis second. I have written a host-health script so
that question has a cheap answer next time instead of a guess.

THE FIX

Every worker thread now sets itself to nice 19 - the bottom of the scheduler.

Why this is the right lever rather than "use fewer threads":

  * When the box is otherwise idle, a niced thread still gets the whole core.
    The 2.6x speedup is untouched.
  * When anything interactive wants CPU - your mail VM, nginx, the web UI -
    it wins instantly, without the research job having to notice or cooperate.
  * On Linux nice is per-thread, so this touches the workers and NOT the
    thread serving the classifier's own web interface.

Verified rather than assumed: with a worker running, exactly one thread of the
process sits at nice 19 and every other thread is at 0. There is a test in the
suite that asserts it, so it cannot quietly regress.

Deployed. Run 6 has been re-fired (bracketlab-20260728-1010) and this email is
being sent WHILE it runs at full tilt - if you are reading it, the fix works
under exactly the load that broke it.

ONE THING I DID NOT TOUCH

Your mail VM has been sitting at ~53% average CPU for five days, and the host
shows an unusually high interrupt rate. That may be completely normal for it
and I have no baseline to compare against. I did not restart it, tune it, or
change anything on it - it is a production service and that is your call, not
a decision for an overnight loop. Flagging it in case it is news to you.
