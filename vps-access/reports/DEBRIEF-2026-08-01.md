# Debrief — the whole picture, in plain words
2026-08-01. Written for the owner's catch-up; no term of art appears
without an everyday translation, and most don't appear at all.

## 1. What we are actually trying to do

Teach six small statistical models to read a coin's recent price-and-
volume behaviour, let them vote on whether the coin goes up or down over
the next day or few, and only trade when enough of them agree. Then —
the hard part, and the part that has consumed this week — build a
measuring bench honest enough to tell whether the votes actually know
anything, before any money follows them.

## 2. The story so far, in five acts

ACT ONE — the old bench was broken. The previous test rig (the
"interlaced" design) claimed to train the models two different ways for
variety. It never did: the second way was the first way with a haircut.
Half the committee were near-copies of the other half, so agreement
counts were inflated. When you got suspicious, we dug, confirmed it, and
tore it out. We also proved, by re-cutting the same history at different
random positions, that its verdicts flipped sign depending on WHERE the
windows happened to land. Every "result" from that era is retired.

ACT TWO — your plan became the new bench. What you described — walk the
training forward through history in chunks; after each chunk of
training, pick the voting rule on a test stretch; then check that pick
on a holdout stretch it has never seen; repeat to the present — is
exactly what got built. Each coin's history yields 25-45 of these
train / pick / check cycles ("folds"), so a setup's verdict is a
distribution over many eras, not one lucky window. Before it ran for
real, a code review you pre-authorized found a serious flaw (the models
were accidentally shown their own exam answers during training — caught
because a fake coin with a known answer failed its test), plus seven
smaller ones. All fixed; the fake-coin test then passed all its checks.

ACT THREE — the first real run (WF1). All 17 coins, four chunk lengths,
two decision styles: 136 combinations, every fold of every coin's
history. Raw scores looked pleasant — 22 combinations made money fold
after fold. But our own pre-declared check showed most of that was just
coins rising: only 7 also beat simply holding the coin. And a deeper
worry stood: nobody knew how many of 136 would "pass" by pure chance.

ACT FOUR — the luck machine. We built a twin of the bench that is
identical in every way except one: inside each fold, the committee's
votes are slid off their dates, so they keep their personality but know
nothing about the market. Whatever THAT twin earns is the definition of
luck for this machinery. First it had to prove itself on the fake coin
with a planted pattern: real votes harvested the pattern (+$310), the
scrambled votes destroyed it (−$95). Only then did it run on real data.

ACT FIVE — the sobering scoreboard, twice over. On pure-luck votes, 5 of
136 combinations still pass the same two rules our 7 real survivors
pass — and that 5 repeated exactly on a second independent scramble. So
counting passers cannot tell our board from luck. A sharper comparison
(same coin, same 8-week stretch, real votes' money minus luck votes'
money, fold by fold) shows the real board ahead: 28 combinations beat
luck on matched stretches, against a luck-beats-luck baseline of 19.
Ahead by 47% — consistently — but not by enough to clear the safety
margin we set in advance. Two more luck runs are in flight right now so
that margin can be MEASURED rather than guessed. That is today's work.

## 3. What the numbers say right now — and what they don't

SAY: the measuring bench works (proven on fake coins with known
answers, twice). The real committee beats pure luck modestly and
repeatedly. The most alive corners: ATOM on 1-day chunks (+$21 per fold
over luck, ahead in 26 of 39 folds), the DOT family, UNI (5 of its 8
cuts ahead of luck). One old favourite, AVAX, turned out to be pure
luck-shaped — every bit of its apparent skill was achievable by
scrambled votes. Six coins (SOL, XRP, LTC + three others) show nothing
under any cut.

DON'T SAY: that anything is tradeable. Nothing has been selected,
nothing retired. If tonight's measured margin says the board's lead
over luck is real, the strong corners go to replication — proving
themselves again on data and rules they weren't fitted to. If it says
the lead is within luck's own wobble, the honest conclusion is that
this committee design's edge is too small for even a good bench to
see, and redesign proceeds knowing that.

## 4. The standing disciplines (why you can trust the reads)

- Every run's reading rules are written and committed BEFORE it fires,
  so results can't be talked into something after the fact.
- No new run until the previous one has a written audit hunting for
  flaws in the measurement itself (enforced by a gate, not goodwill).
- Every threshold is labelled measured-or-guessed; guessed ones get
  replaced by measured ones (happening today), never obeyed blindly.
- Nothing is accepted OR rejected on one measurement. Acceptance needs
  replication; rejection needs replication or a mechanism.
- Every trap we've ever fallen into is a numbered register entry with a
  test that has been WATCHED catching the fault (64 entries and
  counting).
- While any big run is computing, nothing may quietly change the data
  it reads — downloads pause or refuse until it finishes.
- The live paper books (DOT/AVAX tracker, DOGE book) are frozen and
  untouched by all of this.
