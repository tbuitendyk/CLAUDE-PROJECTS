1. What this tool is

A self-hosted laboratory that hunts for predictive structure in Binance hourly data — and is built, above all, to not fool its operator. It cuts history into fixed-shape chunks, scores each chunk by what the trade pair did after the chunk ended, trains a classifier on the older 80% of samples, and reports how well it predicted the most recent 20% it never saw. Every instrument carries its own honesty rails: best-constant baselines, label-shift null calibration, pre-registered decision rules, and explicit "no signal found" verdicts.

  • No AI anywhere in the loop. The only network traffic is downloading public Binance candle archives; training and scoring are deterministic local arithmetic (a pure-JS softmax regression and boosted trees with zero imports). Every result is reproducible by recomputation.
  • Data comes exclusively from Binance's public bulk-data portal (data.binance.vision — keyless monthly/daily zips) plus its keyless REST mirror for the current partial month. Hourly spot klines, pruned to time / open / high / low / close / quote volume, cached on disk forever.
  • Three tabs. Research holds the single-run form and the batch screens. Bracket lab is the execution-permutation system — combo sweeps scored through a bracket-order simulator, nulls only on survivors. Paper books holds the live, frozen, pre-registered experiments — a different category of work, deliberately kept out of the way.
  • All times are UTC.

2. Anatomy of a run (the form)

  • Dormant range ±% — the do-nothing band. A period whose scoring move stays inside it is class 0; beyond it, +1 up or −1 down. auto — balance the classes replaces the manual band with the 33rd percentile of the |scoring move| computed on training samples only, so the three classes split near-evenly and nothing about the test window leaks in.
  • Trade pair / Compare pair — the pair being predicted and the pair supplying market context (default BTCUSDT). Both pairs' candles feed the features.
  • Start / End month, all loaded data — the history window; "all loaded" uses everything cached on the server for those pairs.
  • 24/5 — weekday chunks only — daily shapes only: keeps just the chunks whose feature days AND entry land Mon–Fri (UTC), insulating training from weekend microstructure. Costs samples (a 3-day chunk keeps only Monday starts). Ignored for the weekly shape, which always spans weekends.
  • Chunk shape, Features, Model, Decision — see the next three sections.
  • Load Data vs Run Classifier — Load Data prefetches the months for the two pairs into the server cache; Run uses the cache and fetches anything still missing on the fly. The "Available data on server" grid shows every pair/month already cached.

3. Chunk shapes & scoring

The geometry decides how samples are cut and what "the move" means. Nothing inside the chunk overlaps the scoring window — the label always comes from strictly after the chunk ends.

Features

  • Compressed (default) — 44 engineered numbers per chunk: per asset, the daily returns, total return, hourly volatility and its late-vs-early shift, trend slope and acceleration, max drawdown/run-up, range, last-24h/6h momentum, daily-volume concentration; plus 4 cross-asset features (relative return, relative last-24h, relative volatility, return correlation). Few enough that the model cannot memorize ~300 weeks — training accuracy stays meaningful.
  • Raw hourly — all 1,920 numbers (prices normalized to the chunk's first open, volume to its chunk mean). Kept for A/B comparison; expect memorization at feasible data sizes. Only the single-run form and the legacy quick screen honor it.

4. Training honesty

  • Logistic (linear) — pure-JS multinomial softmax regression. The L2 strength λ is chosen from a ladder validated on the chronological tail of the training set; the ladder auto-extends when the top λ wins, so a chosen λ is always an interior optimum. Iterations auto-stop on convergence (the loss is convex — cycles are not a quality knob).
  • Boosted trees (nonlinear) — small trees on thresholds and interactions. Rounds ARE a real quality knob, so they are tuned automatically: train on the older three-quarters of the training window, watch log-loss on the newest quarter, keep the best round count.
  • The majority reference — every validation table states what always guessing the training-majority class would have scored. Ladder rows at or below it are the model converging to the prior, not finding signal.

5. Decision modes

  • Argmax (3-class) — classic prediction: the most probable of −1 / 0 / +1 wins. The right mode for narrow bands where all three classes are live possibilities.
  • Directional hunter (±1 only, τ by $) — built for WIDE dormant bands (e.g. ±10%) where most periods are 0 and "always predict 0" would be rewarded. Training weights the rare ±1 samples up; the trade call compares P(+1) vs P(−1) only, standing aside when the winner's probability is below a threshold τ. τ comes from a fixed menu scored by paper P&L on the validation tail — dollars, not accuracy, pick how trigger-happy to be, and the test window never influences it. 0 is never "predicted"; it's what happens when conviction is absent. τ = 0 means always-in: trade every period, direction only.

6. Reading a single-run report

  • The tiles — test accuracy (with held-out sample count), the majority-class baseline (always guess the most common class), the random baseline (33.3%), the one-shot paper P&L, and the training-window size. The verdict line under them states plainly whether the model beat both baselines, barely edged the majority, or found nothing — "that is an honest result, not a bug."
  • Data section — candle counts and gap-fills per pair, labelable chunks (with how many were dropped over gaps or missing label data), feature count, the band actually used (auto shows the derived ±%), and the class distribution overall / train / test.
  • Hyperparameter section — the λ ladder (logreg) or boosting-rounds summary (boost), each against the majority reference; for the directional hunter, the chosen τ, the class weights, and the full τ-vs-validation-dollars ladder.
  • What the model leaned on — top features by |weight| (logreg, standardized scale) or by split-gain share (boost). Only trust these if the test accuracy itself beats the baselines.
  • Confusion matrix & per-class metrics — rows = actual, columns = predicted, heat-shaded; precision / recall / F1 per class with supports.
  • Held-out periods, one by one — every test sample with actual, predicted, the model's three probabilities, the realized move, and a one-shot $100 paper trade at the geometry's own entry/exit candles. The paper total is dominated by a few big-move periods — treat it as the dollars reality-check, not the ranking metric.

7. Screens (batch)

Three instruments, all persisting server-side and re-readable from the Saved screens picker. While a screen runs, the view auto-polls every 5 seconds; Refresh forces an update or re-renders after switching screens. Stop jobs is the kill switch: it stops the running screen at its current run and aborts in-flight training within seconds (even with the CPU cap at OFF); completed runs and saved results are kept, and the screen is marked cancelled.

Quick screen (legacy) — 17 pairs × 2 models, one spec each, full view only: the original pre-consensus pulse-check (34 runs). It is the only screen honoring raw features, but it has no feature views, no vote/gate books and NO null support — never a basis for belief. For anything you might act on, use consensus or meta-lens.

8. The consensus screen

  • Per-pair columns — specs done; how many beat their best-constant baseline (positive true edge) and the resulting consensus fraction; the median spec's true edge, balanced accuracy and paper dollars (medians are immune to one lucky or broken cell); then the tradable numbers: vote P&L (wins/trades — click "trades" for the period-by-period history) and vote accuracy with its edge over the best constant; the super 6/8 book (absolute count, not plurality — 5 up vs 3 down stands aside), aimed at fee drag: fewer trades, each backed by broad agreement.
  • The gate ladder — the agreement gradient at quorums 5, 6, 7 and 8 of 8: each rung's book, win rate and trades. When 2+ pairs carry ladders, the screen auto-computes the pre-registered replication test: in how many pairs does win rate rise from 5-of-8 to unanimous (chance 50%), and in how many is it strictly monotone across all four rungs (chance 1 in 8 — the rungs are nested subsets, not independent orderings), each with an exact binomial p. Those two numbers are the whole result; the audit matrix deliberately carries no P&L so it can't be shopped for candidates.
  • Highlighted rows — consensus ≥ 62.5% with positive median true edge. A shortlist heuristic, not a verdict — the nulls decide what's real.

9. Null calibration (label-shift)

  • Exceed rates ≈ empirical p-values — the share of distinct null reruns that matched or beat the real run: on consensus fraction (the primary), on vote-book dollars, on the super book, and per gate-ladder rung (each rung judged against its own noise floor). 0% = noise never faked it that well.
  • Distinct rotations are finite — a pair only has ~(samples − buffer) distinct rotations. Requests beyond the ceiling hit duplicate rotations, which are collapsed into single samples rather than double-counted; the denominator always shows the true distinct count. The max button computes the exact ceiling for the pairs listed and fills it in.
  • Read the medians too — a 0% exceed rate means something different when null books traded as often as the real one (the real book's trades were better) than when noise almost never fired at all (reaching agreement is itself the rare event). The null tables state median null P&L and trades for exactly this reason.
  • Cost — every shift adds 8 more runs per pair; deep nulls (200–1000 shifts) are for single-pair confirmation, not broad sweeps. All work obeys the CPU cap.
  • Family-wise honesty — a low exceed rate on one pair drawn from a many-look hunt does not survive correction across the looks. The project's rule (VERDICTS.md in the repo): deep nulls are read against the declared denominator of the campaign that produced the candidate, and nothing earns belief, a book, or capital directly.

10. The meta-lens screen (two-stage)

  • Force all 8 on zero passing — optional agreement-only mode: continue with ALL 8 lenses as the committee and let stage 2 pick the threshold. Motivated by DOT, where every individual lens was dead yet the 6-of-8 agreement gate survived a 1000-shift null at 2% — a pair's signal can live in agreement alone. Forced runs are stamped (forced: all 8) so they can never pass as selective ones; nulls inherit the setting.
  • Interlaced split (purged) — instead of chronological halves, alternate 49-day blocks between A and B so both span the whole history (both DOT and DOGE showed the raw edge flipping sign between eras). Whole weeks keep weekday composition identical; the odd multiple drifts across the calendar so blocks never line up with month-end structure. Because neighbouring chunks overlap, the last 5 chunks of every block are PURGED (2 for weekly geometry) — without that, interlacing would leak training data into the scoring set. The test window stays chronological-last either way.
  • Reading it — per pair: lenses passed, committee size, chosen threshold, and the one-shot test verdict (trades, P&L, gross/trade, accuracy and edge), with P&L and edge exceed rates from the full-recipe nulls. The stage-1 scoreboard and stage-2 menu render underneath so the recipe's choices are auditable.

11. The staged-picks screen (permutation matrix)

A candidate-forging workflow where you are the selection mechanism, stage by stage, with every pick persisted server-side. Stage 1 (0 nulls by design) runs every pair's 8 specs under both training windows — the full training window and the interlaced-purged window (49-day blocks, overlapping tails dropped). Those two are all the five protocol permutations (classic + four meta-lens variants) amount to at the spec level: lens selection, thresholds and force-all are committee machinery, and in this workflow the committee is yours. 16 members per asset, all sharing the asset's band and test window so their calls stay period-aligned.

  • The stages — checkbox ONE asset → checkbox a member subset → read the quorum menu (1-of-n … n-of-n, best dollars first, with accuracy and trade-hit alongside) → checkbox the rungs to keep → fire the null on that frozen selection. The view names the stage you're on; selections survive reloads.
  • The quorum rule — net direction wins: the majority side among the committee trades when its ABSOLUTE count reaches the rung; tied up/down stands aside.
  • The null — retrains only the picked members per label rotation, judges only the kept rungs (each against its own noise floor), banks rotations LIVE so the exceed table fills in while it runs, and reports P&L exceed, accuracy exceed and null medians. Read it as a conditional calibration: your hand-picks cannot be replayed inside it, so it is never a clean p-value — the ledger's denominator applies, and a forward book remains the only clean test.

12. The Bracket lab (execution permutations)

Its own tab: a deliberately promiscuous search machine that sweeps asset combos × option branches × an execution menu, with a mechanical, stamped selection rule — and no nulls in the sweep at all. The philosophy: shop widely and mechanically, then null-test only the survivors, letting the null replay everything downstream of the combo.

  • Combos — checkbox any subset of singles (trade asset alone; no cross view exists there), doubles (trade + one context — BTC is just one candidate), and triples (trade + two contexts), over a universe you type (empty = all 17). Labels always come from the trade asset, so every layout of a combo shares band and test window.
  • Permute toggles — each option runs as set or sweeps its whole declared menu as extra branches: chunk shape (all 5), decision (both), band (auto / ±3 / ±5 / ±8), 24/7 vs 24/5 (weekly skips the redundant branch). The full unit count — combos × branches — and the projected wall-clock render the moment you hit Start.
  • Gates — always (both rails every period — the control), on-active (both rails only when the committee calls a move), direction-filtered (only the rail matching the call). The committee is the majority vote in the slim stage and every quorum rung 1..n at the promoted stage — all swept, never chosen by hand.
  • vs control — and why it proves nothing on a search board — the column is the row's net minus the best always cell on the same combo and branch. On the leaderboard that difference cannot come out negative: the row won a search whose menu already contained every always cell, so a gated winner has beaten the control by construction and an always winner IS the control (shown as “—”). Read it as how much gating won by, never as evidence that gating works. The same subtraction becomes real in the replication table, where the cell is declared before the run and cannot be swapped for a better one, while the control still gets its full 35-cell search — so a positive there is a conservative result.
  • Slim-then-promote — every unit first runs a slim grid (logreg over the views, full window); the top K by the declared rule are re-run on the full 16-member grid. The rule is fixed: best net dollars over the whole menu, minimum-trade floor, ties to fewer trades.
  • Honest fills — hourly bars can't order intra-bar touches, so any bar that spans both rails resolves AGAINST the book (entered, then stopped in the same bar) and is counted in the ambig column. Results are conservative by construction; a fat ambig count means d is small relative to hourly ranges.
  • Live status — phase (slim / promote / null), units and trainings done over the full denominator, rate per minute, seconds per training, elapsed, ETA at current pace, failures — and the leaderboard reranking as combos complete. Interim numbers are for watching, not for stopping early; the promote rule fires mechanically at completion.
  • The null, and what it can and cannot say — pick a promoted survivor and fire: per rotation the null retrains the full grid and gets EVERY freedom the machine had downstream of the combo — the whole menu, all quorum rungs, the same best-cell rule. Two readings render live: best-of-menu exceed (the search-replayed calibration) and same-config exceed (conditional). What is NOT replayed — and cannot be, at tens of thousands of trainings per rotation — is the combo search itself: that multiplicity stays on the stamp and must be read against it. As everywhere: a forward book is the only clean test.

13. Paper books

Live, frozen, pre-registered experiments — the forward tests that decide whether a screened result was real. Nothing here retrains, and the rules cannot move after the first live period. Protocols and amendment logs live in the repo (TRACKER.md, TRACKER-DOGE.md, VERDICTS.md).

Live tracker — DOT/AVAX weekly (Book 1)

  • Frozen consensus grids (8 specs per pair, trained through 2026-06, never retrained) predict every week's Tue→Thu move. Majority vote is the headline; ties stand aside. $100 per order, entry Tue 03:00 open, exit Thu 15:00 open, $0.50 friction per leg (the books' permanently declared stress rate — research screens moved to $0.25/trip in 2026-07, so compare across that boundary gross, not net).
  • LIVE vs SEEDED — a prediction is LIVE if recorded before its outcome window opens (Thu 12:00 UTC); the anti-peeking guarantee is determinism — every prediction is a pure function of the frozen weights and Binance's published candles, so it can be verified by recomputation. Backfilled weeks are flagged SEEDED and reported alongside but never count toward the verdict.
  • Verdict — at 26 LIVE weeks per pair: pass = vote-book P&L > 0 AND accuracy above the best-constant baseline of those same weeks. Both pairs are reported regardless. Each of the 8 specs also runs its own $100 book for method comparison.

Live tracker II — DOGE daily-3d (Book 2)

  • A second, fully independent book (separate module and state — the DOT/AVAX record cannot be perturbed by it). Frozen 8-spec grid predicts every day's 41-hour move: entry 01:00 UTC the day after the 72-hour chunk closes, exit 18:00 the following day.
  • Five rules declared in advance, all five reported — the majority vote plus quorums 5, 6, 7 and 8 of 8 — so nothing is promoted after the fact and no multiplicity correction is owed. The backtest gradient to beat: win rate 53.7 / 57.4 / 62.6 / 67.3% across the four quorums.
  • Positions overlap (a signal a day against a 41-hour hold), so implied capital is ~$200–300, not $100.

New paper books — the engine

  • Draft first — creating a book shows the data coverage, the would-be adaptive band and the full generated protocol with nothing trained or frozen. Drafts can be discarded freely.
  • Declare & initialize — trains the 8 specs, freezes weights, band and rules, and starts the record. From that moment the engine refuses all changes: the only verbs left are view and retire. Retiring closes the record permanently (failure-by-default unless net-positive on every primary rule at retirement).
  • Permanent numbering — every declared book gets the next number, and the count of declared books is the honest denominator against any future winner. The form fields (pair, geometry, band, history start, training cutoff, horizon in live periods, the rule set, and the rationale) all go into the declaration verbatim.
  • The two original books run on their own frozen code, untouched by this engine.

14. Data, CPU & job control

  • The cache — every pair/month ever downloaded is parsed once and kept forever in data/cache/; the "Available data on server" grid shows the current coverage. The current partial month comes from the keyless REST mirror when reachable.
  • CPU button (header) — service-wide cap on heavy work (training, boosting, screens): cycles 100 / 90 / 75 / 50 / 25 / 10% / OFF. Applies LIVE to already-running work within seconds; results are identical at any setting — only speed changes. OFF parks heavy work in place until turned back up. Stored server-side, so every browser sees the same setting.
  • Stop jobs — aborts the running screen and any in-flight training within seconds, even while parked at OFF. Finished runs and saved results survive; the screen is marked cancelled.
  • Jobs poll — long work runs as server-side jobs the page polls; closing the tab never kills a screen.

15. Glossary

Dormant band
The ±% do-nothing zone. Moves inside it score 0; beyond it, ±1. "Auto" = the 33rd percentile of |move| on training samples only, balancing the classes.
Chunk / geometry
One sample: a fixed window of hourly candles from both pairs (weekly 8-day or daily 1–4-day), labeled by the trade pair's move strictly after it ends.
Spec
One method permutation: a feature view × a model. The consensus grid runs 8 per pair.
View
Feature slice: full 44 / prices-only / volume-only / cross-asset-only.
Best constant
Accuracy of always guessing the test window's most common class, in hindsight — the toughest do-nothing competitor.
True edge
Test accuracy − best constant. Positive = beat the smartest lazy strategy.
Balanced accuracy
Mean of the three per-class hit rates; 33.3% = chance for ANY know-nothing strategy regardless of class mix.
Consensus fraction
Specs with positive true edge ÷ specs done — agreement across methods, a pair's headline score.
Vote book
Majority vote of the 8 specs each test period (ties stand aside), trading $100 at the geometry's entry/exit candles. The tradable number.
Super 6/8 / quorum qN
Trades only when ≥N specs call the SAME direction (absolute count, not plurality). The agreement gradient is the ladder q5→q8.
Null shift / exceed rate
Full rerun with circularly time-rotated labels; the exceed rate is the share of distinct rotations matching or beating the real run — an empirical p-value.
Distinct rotations
The finite ceiling of usable label shifts (~samples − buffer); duplicates collapse into single null samples.
τ (tau)
The directional hunter's stand-aside threshold on the winning direction's probability, tuned by validation paper dollars. τ=0 = always in.
Lens
Meta-lens name for a spec when treated as a committee member subject to stage-1 selection.
Purged interlacing
Alternating 49-day A/B blocks with the last chunks of each block dropped so overlapping chunks can't leak across the boundary.
LIVE / SEEDED
Tracker provenance: recorded before the outcome window opens vs backfilled after. Only LIVE periods count toward a verdict.
Paper book
$100-per-order simulated record at declared friction. Research friction $0.25/round trip (since 2026-07-26); the frozen live books keep their declared $0.50/leg stress rate forever.
Combo / branch / unit
Bracket lab: a combo is the asset set (single/double/triple); a branch is one setting of the permutable options; a unit is one combo × branch — the denominator the counter counts through.
OCO bracket
Stop-entries at p±d; the first rail filled opens the position, the other rail becomes its stop; time exit at t. Direction chosen by the market.
Ambiguous bar
An hourly bar spanning both rails — fill order unknowable, always resolved against the book and counted.
Slim / promote
Bracket lab staging: a cheap logreg-view grid sweeps every unit; the declared top-K re-run on the full 16-member grid with quorum rungs.
Search-replayed exceed
Null reading where each rotation gets the machine's full downstream freedom (menu, quorums, selection rule) — the honest calibration for a mechanically-shopped survivor, conditional on its combo.
Training windows
The two per-spec realities the protocol permutations collapse to: the full training window, and the interlaced-purged window (49-day blocks, overlapping tails dropped).
Horizon
The pre-registered number of LIVE settled periods after which a book's verdict is computed once and never moves again.
