## S4 — PARKED, because the premise was false, and I had shipped the false part

The step was "the engine's third ceiling becomes a control". Tracing where to
plumb it found that **the ceiling governs nothing the owner runs.**

- `CLASS_WEIGHT_CAP` lives in `trainMember`, in `lib/bracket.js`.
- Nothing in `lib/` calls `trainMember`. Grepped the whole repo: it is
  referenced from `tests/test-bracket.js` and from comments, and nowhere else.
- The three-stage engine trains through `trainProbMember` in
  `lib/stagework.js`, which calls `tuneAndTrain` with `exampleWeights` and
  nothing else. `classWeights` there defaults to null.

**So no sweep the owner launches weighs a rare answer up by anything at all.**

### What I shipped this morning was false

3.119.0 put this on the Coins screen: *"A side thinner than 1.7% of a part will
not be rescued by weighting — that is where the engine's own ceiling of 20
across 3 answers stops being able to correct the imbalance."* Every clause of it
is about code the owner never executes. I derived it carefully from the engine
rather than typing it, wrote a test pinning it, aimed a mutation guard at it,
and put it in front of them — and it was about the wrong function the whole
time. Deriving a number from the code is not the same as checking the code runs.

### What was done, and what was not

**Done, because the screen was lying and I put the lie there:** the sentence is
replaced with the plainer and more useful truth — a thin side gets no help at
all, at any thinness. The helper that computed the level is deleted, the runner
no longer serves it, `COINS.md` section 8 and finding 1 in section 14 are
corrected, and the test that pinned the false figure is replaced by one that
pins the fact: while the trainer passes no class weights, the screen may not say
weighting will rescue anything. It fails loudly in BOTH directions — if the
engine ever gains class weighting and the screen does not say so, it says the
two have come apart the other way round.

**Not done, PARKED for the owner:** exposing the ceiling. It would be a control
over dead code, which is worse than no control. The real question underneath is
theirs and it is large: the two-set design in COINS.md section 2 assumes
something handles the thin side, and nothing does. Whether the engine should
balance a rare answer changes how every model is fitted.

**Also parked, reported not touched:** `trainMember` and `predictMember` in
`lib/bracket.js` appear to be the older engine's trainer with no caller in
`lib/`. Whether that is dead code to retire is a separate question and not this
batch's.


## S6 — the two numbers exist, and one half of the success rule cannot be checked yet

**Built.** Every trained unit's record now carries, beside what it was trained
under: the biggest weight **before** the ceiling clipped anything, and how many
chunks were held at the ceiling. Both are on the stage 1 and stage 2 tables,

The old number is renamed rather than reused. It was `biggest` and it was the

ceiling   5: biggest BEFORE cap 22.47   held at ceiling  1   biggest kept  5.00
ceiling  20: biggest BEFORE cap 22.47   held at ceiling  1   biggest kept 20.00
